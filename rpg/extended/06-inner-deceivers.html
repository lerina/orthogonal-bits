CHAPTER 06

THE INNER DECEIVERS

Six faces on a die. Six patterns of self-sabotage. For the first time, "which one's in the way today" is a draw, not a guess.

COMPLETING THE SET

Why Six, Why Now

The base rulebook's Adversary table (Section 7) has three types. The Salvaged Systems side text mapped five real-life "deceivers" onto that three-part frame. This chapter goes one step further: a sixth deceiver, and all six lined up one-to-one against the faces of the Digit-Sum Draw's D6. When a real-life quest (Chapter 05) stalls and it isn't obvious what's actually in the way, draw — the table below answers it.

THE ROSTER

The Six Deceivers

1

THE JUDGEMENTAL

HUNTER

The eternal critic — what you did, didn't do, should have done. Grows stronger every time you lose sight of what's actually in your control.

2

THE VICTIMIZER

ENFORCER

The excuse-generator, the willpower-drainer. Grows stronger every time you give up or stop trying.

3

THE RINGMASTER

HUNTER

Productivity guilt — the unsustainable drive to keep saying yes. Grows stronger every time your worth gets tied to your output.

4

THE NEGLECTOR

HUNTER

Wins when you feel insecure and go looking for approval. Grows stronger every time you prioritize everyone else's needs over your own.

5

THE MISGUIDED PROTECTOR

WATCHER

Sells avoidance as safety — exaggerates the difficulty of starting at all. "Safe" but stuck. Grows stronger every time you're paralyzed into no action.

6

THE SIREN

HUNTER

The endless-scope dreamer — a project with no deadline, forever almost-starting. Grows stronger every time the plan gets bigger instead of the work getting done.

THE FIRST MOVE, EVERY TIME

The Antidote

Whichever deceiver comes up, the same First Move works: create distance between yourself and the voice before answering it.

  1. Notice you're under attack — name the deceiver out loud.
  2. Call it out, then choose not to listen to it.
  3. Answer the specific argument it's making:
    • Safe and stuck? Ask if this is actually inside your control, then take one small, imperfect action right now.
    • Busy pleaser? Ask whether it's who you are or what you do that actually matters here — and whether your boundaries are even up.
    • Seeking approval? Ask whether this choice is really an avoidance trap wearing a nicer name.

Underneath all six: what's done is done, what's next is a choice you make right now, the process is the goal rather than perfection, and the only question worth asking in the moment is whether what you're doing is helping or harming you. Two people can look at the same setback and see two different things — one only the obstacle, the other the opening it creates.
