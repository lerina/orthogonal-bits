CHAPTER 02

CHARACTER & ALTER-EGO GENERATOR

The Rule of Three is gone. In its place: two oracle tables, an open Trait list, and a set of questions that turn a handful of draws into a person.

WHAT REPLACES IT

Beyond the Rule of Three

The base game caps a character at 3 Gear, 2 Cyberware, 1 Style so the whole sheet fits in a breath. Extended Edition trades that ceiling for a single guiding principle instead:

If you can't picture using it in a scene, cut it. Keep drawing and adding Traits until the character feels complete — not until you hit a number.

Traits replace the old Gear/Cyberware/Style split with three open-ended flavors. Draw as many as the character needs:

CAPABILITY

What they can do

Old Gear and Cyberware entries both live here now — an item, an implant, a contact, a skill.

VOICE

How they do it

The old Style slot, no longer limited to one — a character can carry more than one way of being themselves.

BAGGAGE

What complicates them

New in this edition — debts, enemies, secrets, and burdens that give the Digit-Sum Draw's "but" and "and" results something specific to land on.

ORACLE, PART ONE

Good At / Known For / Have

Draw once per column using the Digit-Sum Draw's D6 lookup from Chapter 01. Each result becomes one Capability Trait — invent the concrete detail that fits.

AFTER THE DRAWS

Fleshing Out the Alter-Ego

Name them, then answer whatever subset of these actually sparks something — skip the rest:

  • What do they do? What do they wear? What do they eat?
  • Who do they spend time with? What does their daily routine look like?
  • What habits do they practice? What does their life look like?
  • What values do you share with them — and which would you like to?

If the alter-ego is also gamifying your real life

  • Why do I want to reinvent myself?
  • What habits and choices would I have to say yes to?
  • What habits and choices would I have to say no to?
  • Will I stay the course even when it gets rough?

This last set matters more once Chapter 05 (Real-Life Missions) ties the character's progress to things you actually do — the alter-ego isn't just a mask, it's a target you're drawing toward.
