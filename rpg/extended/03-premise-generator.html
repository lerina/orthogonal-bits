CHAPTER 03

THE PREMISE GENERATOR

Six draws instead of three fixed hooks. Same One Corp, One Gang, One Bar world — an oracle for when you want a mission the book didn't already write for you.

BEFORE YOU DRAW

Six Draws, One Premise

The base game's three Story Hooks are fixed and ready to use. This chapter replaces them with two three-column tables — draw once per column using the Chapter 01 Digit-Sum Draw, and the six results assemble into a premise nobody wrote in advance.

A

Table A — the setup

Who's bringing you the job, what they want done, and why it matters to them.

B

Table B — the target

What you're actually going after, how you heard about it, and what's standing in the way.

"An old mentor asks you to pull someone out of a corporate datacenter — for revenge, not payment — based on a tip from someone you used to trust, across a stretch of city with nowhere to hide along the way."
WHAT TO DO WITH IT

Feed It Into Quest Deconstruction

A generated premise isn't a finished scene — it's raw material. Run it straight through the core rulebook's six-step Quest Deconstruction (Section 12):

  1. Name the Target from the Mission and Target columns.
  2. Find the Obstacle — already sitting in the Complication column.
  3. Set the Stakes using the Incentive column as your starting motive.
  4. Pick an Adversary Type — most Complications map cleanly to a Watcher, Enforcer, or Hunter.
  5. Define the First Move — usually the Seed, dramatized: how you actually approach what you heard about.
  6. Attach a Reward — your own call, same as always.
Six draws, six steps — the tables hand you the raw material, Quest Deconstruction turns it into something playable in the same breath.
