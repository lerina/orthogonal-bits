CHAPTER 08 · FINAL

RUNNING THE GAME — TWISTS, MOOD & DIRECTION

Four quick-reference tables for keeping a running session honest and occasionally surprising — all of them built on the exact same six-outcome engine from Chapter 01.

THE SAME TABLE, ASKED DIFFERENTLY

Binary(ish) Questions

When a scene needs a closed yes/no answer instead of an action resolution — does the door open, is the contact still alive, did the tip check out — ask the question and run the Digit-Sum Draw exactly as in Chapter 01. It's the same six-outcome table, just aimed at the world instead of at what your character is doing.

"CAUSE" RESULTS (1–3)

Yes, and / Yes / Yes, but

The answer is yes, at increasing cost. A "Yes, but" is a small complication; treat it as narrative weight, never a number to subtract.

"TAKE" RESULTS (4–6)

No, but / No / No, and

The answer is no, with increasing fallout. A "No, and" is the scene actively getting worse — again, narrated, not tallied.

No harm points, no hidden pool — the distance from the center of the table already tells you how big the moment is. Play that distance, don't track it.
WHEN THE SAME FACE COMES UP TWICE

Doubles and the Twist Table

Draw the same D6 result twice in a row — on any table in this edition — and the story itself moves, not just the current action. Draw once more and read it here instead:

D6 → the twist
D6The Twist
1A third party appears.
2The location itself changes.
3An unexpected encounter helps you.
4A physical complication hinders you.
5An emotional gut-punch changes your goal.
6An object ends the scene outright.

This is the closest thing this edition has to a built-in GM — a way for a solo session to genuinely surprise itself instead of only ever escalating along the line you already expected.

WHEN YOU DON'T KNOW THE TONE

Scene Mood Draw

Stuck on tone rather than content? Draw a D6:

D6 → next scene's tone
D6Next Scene
1–3Dramatic — stakes up, something's actively wrong.
4–5Quiet — a breather, low pressure.
6Meanwhile — cut away entirely to another thread.
ACTING OUTSIDE YOUR SPECIALTY

Distracted vs. Directed

When a character attempts something outside their usual Capability, check whether the single digit from the Digit-Sum Draw (before the D6 conversion) came out even or odd:

EVEN

Distracted

Something else has their attention mid-action. Narrate the complication as split focus, not incompetence.

ODD

Directed

They're locked in despite this not being their lane. Narrate the outcome as pure will carrying them through.

This changes nothing mechanically — it's a narration lens on top of whatever the D6 already produced, not a separate modifier.
