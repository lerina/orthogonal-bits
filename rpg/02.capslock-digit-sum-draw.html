WHY IT WORKS

Why It's Hat-Shaped, Not Flat

A single raw digit would be evenly likely across the board. This method isn't that — two things stack on top of each other to pull it toward the middle.

SUMMING PULLS TOWARD THE MIDDLE

Adding two digits and reducing behaves like rolling two dice instead of one — there are more ways to land on a middle value than an edge value.

THE TABLE BANDS ARE UNEVEN ON PURPOSE

1 and 6 each come from exactly one digit. 2 through 5 each come from two digits — double the odds of the extremes.

The result: a curve that peaks in the middle and tapers at both ends — same shape as reading the total on two six-sided dice, produced from a clock instead.

APPLICATION

Recommended Uses

USE THIS FOR

Middling outcomes as the norm

Skill checks, reaction rolls, oracle answers — anything where total triumph or total disaster should be a rare, memorable surprise, not a routine 1-in-6.

NOT FOR

A flat, even pick among six

"Which of six NPCs shows up" wants equal odds — the banding over-favors the middle. Use raw seconds mod 6, plus 1 instead.

In CAPSLOCK specifically, this is the exact mechanism behind the Six-Degree Draw — reach for it whenever a scene wants the full six-outcome range instead of the base Clock Draw's plain three bands.
