## THE RULE OF THREE

Every character picks:

- **3 Gear** from their archetype's menu
- **2 Cyberware** from their archetype's menu
- **1 Memory Anchor** (free, personal, narrative)

# PART ONE: THE SHARED TOOLKIT

These items are available across archetypes. They are not exclusive to Signal Runners, but Signal Runners are the ones who use them most. The sprawl does not make special equipment for blind people. It makes equipment for people who want to work without looking. And blind people have been using it for decades.

---

## 1.1 Screen Readers & Audio Interfaces

**VoiceWeave** — Bone-conduction auditory interface. Reads text, system output, and metadata aloud in a customized synthetic voice. Up to 900 words per minute. The user chooses the voice. The voice becomes a companion. Some runners name their voices. Some do not. (Analog: VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA, JAWS.)

**Whisper** — Subvocal audio interface. Reads system output directly into the auditory cortex via bone conduction. Silent to everyone else in the room. The user hears the system. The room hears nothing.

**Verse** — Semantic screen reader. Tells you what a screen *is* before it tells you what it says. A menu. A warning. A dead end. A form that wants three fields. Gives the shape before the content. Experienced runners prefer it. Beginners find it confusing. (Analog: advanced screen reader with layout awareness.)

**Tacet** — Reads silences. Tells you what is *not* there. What fields are empty. What processes are missing. What the form does not ask for. The Line considers Tacet the most advanced screen reader in the sprawl. Very few runners can use it. It requires a mind that notices absences. (Fictional.)

**Aria** — Persona voice interface. Not a synthetic voice. A *persona*. Some users pick a calm voice. Some pick a voice that sounds like a friend. Some pick a voice that sounds like nobody they have ever met, because they do not want to be comforted. They want to be told. (Fictional.)

**Loop** — Short-loop auditory buffer. Repeats the last ten seconds of audio. Useful for catching missed details. Dangerous — runners get stuck. The manual warns against it. The manual also says: *if you are stuck, start smaller.* Loop is the opposite of smaller. (Fictional.)

---

## 1.2 Braille & Tactile Interfaces

**Refreshable Braille Display (RBD)** — Standard. A row of pins. Renders text. Read by fingertip. Fast, quiet. (Analog: BrailleNote, HumanWare.)

**Full-Page Braille Slate** — Larger display. Full page at once. Heavy. Expensive. Rare. People who carry them do not leave them behind. (Analog: full-page refreshable displays.)

**Tactile Floor-Plan Overlay** — A mat or board. Renders a space as raised lines. Read by feet or hands. (Analog: tactile maps.)

**Braille Tattoo** — Dermal braille. Raised dots under the skin of the forearm or wrist. Renders short messages. Permanent. Cannot be removed. Cannot be read by anyone who does not know braille. (Fictional.)

**Dermal Braille Patch** — Temporary. Renders live data — system status, proximity alerts, incoming messages. Peels off. Disposable. Expensive. (Fictional.)

**Palm Reader** — Small tactile device. Held in the palm. Renders text on a refreshable surface. Reads by touch. No screen. (Fictional.)

---

## 1.3 Proximity & Navigation

**Sunu-Style Sonar Band** — Wristband. Ultrasonic pulses. Haptic feedback. Feels objects before they reach them. Range: three metres. Vibrates faster as you approach. (Analog: Sunu Band, BuzzClip.)

**Smart Cane** — Cane with ultrasonic sensors and haptic feedback. Detects obstacles above knee level — the things a standard cane misses. Vibrates when it finds something. (Analog: WeWALK.)

**Subdermal Compass** — Micro-implant at the base of the skull. Constant sense of true bearing. No interface. Just knowing. (Fictional.)

**Echo-Sense** — Implant. Maps a space by sound. User clicks or hums. Implant reads echo. The shape of the room appears — not as an image, as a sense of space. (Fictional.)

**Wayfinder** — Navigation implant. Guides through known routes via haptic pulses. Left hand: turn left. Right hand: turn right. Both: stop. Pattern on back: arrived. (Fictional.)

**Trace** — Short-range position tracker. Marks a location with a tiny beacon. User can always find their way back. Range: one kilometre. (Fictional.)

---

## 1.4 Text & Image Access

**OcuRead** — Handheld scanner. Reads printed text aloud. Point at a page. It reads. (Analog: OCR readers, Seeing AI, KNFB Reader.)

**Scene Describe** — Camera-and-audio device. Describes a scene. *There is a person standing by the counter. They are wearing a coat. They are holding something in their left hand.* (Analog: Seeing AI, Be My Eyes.)

**Human Bridge** — A service, not a device. Network of sighted volunteers. User points a camera. A stranger describes what is there. (Analog: Be My Eyes, Aira.)

**Tactile Raster** — Converts images into tactile output. Photograph becomes raised lines and textures. Read by hand. Slow. Imprecise. Better than nothing. (Analog: tactile graphics embossers.)

---

## 1.5 Undistracted-Sight Tools

For sighted users who choose to work without visual rendering. They are not blind. They have chosen the discipline.

**Blinders** — Opaque lenses or light-blocking hood. Removes the visual channel. Forces work by touch and sound. Uncomfortable. Effective. First hour is hardest. (Fictional, based on real training tools.)

**Visual Squelch** — Cyberware. Temporarily suppresses visual cortex input. User can still see, but the brain stops rendering. Everything becomes blur and movement. Useful for Netrunners who want to work without the deck's visual layer. (Fictional.)

**White Room** — Physical space with no visual features. No windows. No decorations. No colour. The room gives nothing to look at. The Line's oldest runners use these for deep work. (Fictional, based on real sensory-neutral spaces.)

# PART THREE: THE SIGNAL RUNNER — DEEP DIVE

---

## 3.1 Three Populations, One Toolkit

The Signal Runner toolkit serves three populations.

**Blind users.** Born blind or blinded. No useful retinal implant. No cortical patch. No neural rendering. The sprawl's medical system has been promising next-generation hardware for decades. It has not arrived. They stopped waiting.

**Low-vision users.** Partial sight. Enough to navigate, not enough to trust. Some use visual aids. Some use audio and tactile. Some use both. They calibrate their toolkit to their remaining vision — and to their need to work without it when the work demands precision.

**Undistracted-sight users.** Fully sighted. Chose to work without visual rendering. Not because they cannot see. Because they have learned — through training, through experience, through the Line's discipline — that the visual channel is a liability. They can see. They choose not to look. The Line calls this *working blind* even though the person is not blind.

All three use the same tools. The reasons differ. The discipline does not.

---

## 3.2 The Instrument: The Terminal

The Signal Runner works at a terminal.

Not a deck. Not a rendering. Not a visual interface. A terminal. A shell. A command line. A braille display and a keyboard and a method.

They are masters of the console. They read systems the way other runners read a cityscape — through text, through output, through the shape of data as it appears in a shell.

### The Tools

**Bash.** The shell. The foundation. Every Signal Runner knows bash the way a driver knows the route.

**grep.** The search tool. The first query. The smallest question. `grep -r "function_name" /path` is the Signal Runner's opening move.

**ripgrep (rg).** Faster grep. Recursive by default. The Signal Runner's preferred search tool.

**find.** Locate files by name, date, size, permission. The Signal Runner's mapping tool.

**awk and sed.** Text processing. The Signal Runner's scissors.

**jq.** JSON query. The Signal Runner reads structured data the way other runners read diagrams.

**strace.** Trace system calls. Watch a process talk to the kernel. The Signal Runner's ear.

**ltrace.** Trace library calls. Watch a process talk to its dependencies.

**lsof.** List open files. What does this process have open. What is it holding.

**netstat / ss.** Network connections. What is listening. What is connected. What is reaching out.

**ps / top / htop.** Process trees. What is running. What is a parent. What is a child.

**Helix (hx).** A modal text editor. Keyboard-only. No mouse. No visual rendering. The Signal Runner's preferred editor. (Or vim. Or nano. The Line argues about this constantly.)

**Obscura.** A headless web browser. Renders nothing. Returns text. The Signal Runner's window into the web without a window.

**curl / wget.** Fetch. The Signal Runner's hands on the network.

**ssh / scp.** Remote access. The Signal Runner's way in.

**tmux / screen.** Terminal multiplexing. Many sessions. One display. The Signal Runner's workspace.

**script.** Record a terminal session. The Signal Runner's log.

**diff / patch.** Compare and change. The Signal Runner's trace tool.

**xxd / hexdump.** Read binary. The Signal Runner's microscope.

**strings.** Extract text from binary. The Signal Runner's archaeology.

**file.** Identify file type. The Signal Runner's first question about an unknown object.

**md5sum / sha256sum.** Hash. Verify. The Signal Runner's integrity check.

**watch.** Repeat a command. Monitor a change. The Signal Runner's patience.

**xargs.** Build commands from output. The Signal Runner's chain.

**tee.** Split output. Send to file and screen. The Signal Runner's two-handed work.

**less / more.** Read long output. The Signal Runner's scroll.

**man / info.** Read the manual. The Signal Runner's first habit.

**history.** Recall commands. The Signal Runner's memory.

**alias / function.** Build personal tools. The Signal Runner's craft.

---

## 3.3 The Workflow

A Signal Runner sits at a terminal.

They run `file` on the unknown object. They run `strings` on the binary. They `grep` for a function name. They `rg` through a directory. They `find` for recently modified files. They `strace` a running process. They `lsof` to see what is open. They `ss` to see what is connected.

They read the output on a braille display. They hear the output through a screen reader. They feel the shape of the system in text.

They ask one question. They get one answer. They ask the next.

They do not see the system. They read it.

---

## 3.4 Why the Terminal

The terminal is not a compromise. It is a discipline.

A visual rendering gives you a model. A terminal gives you the thing itself. `ls` does not show you a beautiful tree. It shows you the files. `ps` does not show you a cityscape. It shows you the processes. `grep` does not show you a highlighted pathway. It shows you the lines that match.

The terminal does not render. It reports.

And a report can be verified. A rendering can only be trusted.

This is why Compile used a terminal. This is why the Signal Runner uses a terminal. Not because they cannot see. Because they refuse to be shown.

---

## 3.5 The Line's Argument

The Line has two traditions and they argue constantly.

The **Netrunner** tradition says: use the deck. Use the rendering. Use the visual model. It is faster. It is more powerful. It is the standard tool of the trade.

The **Signal Runner** tradition says: the rendering is a model, and a model is not the territory, and a runner who trusts a model is a runner who is about to be wrong.

Both are correct. The Netrunner is faster. The Signal Runner is more precise. The Line teaches both. And the Line's oldest runners will tell you that the best runners use both — a deck for speed, a terminal for truth, and the discipline to know which one they are looking at.
