# THE FOUR ARCHETYPES (Expanded to Six)

---

## NETRUNNER — *"the system already told me everything. I just had to listen."*

**Focus:** Technical & Intelligence.

**Gear:** intrusion cyberdeck with neural buffer · signal sniffer · a deck of physical key-cards (for the systems that still use them).

**Cyberware:** Datajack (direct neural interface) · Internal Cache (store and retrieve data without external media).

**Always-On — Deep Read:** once per scene, you can interrogate a system without touching it. Ask one question about a network, device, or piece of code — ownership, last access, hidden layer, active process — and receive a true answer. The system does not know it was read.

---

**The Netrunner is the traditional deck operator.** They wear the rendering. They see the cityscape. They navigate the visual model. They are fast. They are good. They are the ones the sprawl thinks of when it thinks of a hacker.

But the Line's current consensus is that the deck is a structural vulnerability. And the Netrunner is the one who has to live with that.

They are not Compile. Compile removed the deck entirely. The Netrunner keeps it. The Netrunner has to work inside the rendering and verify outside it at the same time. They have two models in their head — what the deck shows them and what the query tells them — and they have to know which one to trust. That is the discipline. That is the craft.

A Netrunner who trusts the rendering is a dead Netrunner. A Netrunner who never uses the rendering is a slow Netrunner. The job is to hold both.

**In the story:** The Netrunner tradition is what Compile left behind. The Line still teaches it. Most runners still use decks. The line from the manual — *establish the relationship before you trust the picture* — is the Netrunner's daily discipline.

**Relationship to other archetypes:** Netrunners and Signal Runners are two expressions of the same craft. Netrunners work the system from inside the rendering. Signal Runners work the system from outside it. They need each other. They argue constantly.

---

## SIGNAL RUNNER — *"I don't need to see the system. I need to know what it does."*

**Focus:** Intelligence & Cool.

**Gear:** terminal with braille display · a notebook and pen (physical, always) · a single small tool of the runner's choice (search, symbol lookup, process trace).

**Cyberware:** none required. Signal Running is a discipline, not an implant. But many Signal Runners carry one piece of cyberware for practical reasons — a datajack for terminal connection, or a memory augment for holding question chains.

**Always-On — Reduce the Search Space:** once per scene, when investigating a system, you may ask the smallest possible question and receive a true answer. The question must be specific. *Where is this defined. What owns it. Who last touched it.* You do not get context. You do not get the answer to the next question. You get one fact. And one fact is enough to narrow the field.

---

**The Signal Runner is Compile's archetype.** They do not wear a deck. They do not trust a rendering. They sit at a terminal with a braille display and a keyboard and a method. They ask one question at a time. They build each next question on the answer they received. They stay uncertain longer than anyone else can tolerate.

They are slower than a Netrunner. They are more careful than a Netrunner. And on the jobs that matter, they are the ones who come back.

The Signal Runner's power is not speed. It is precision. They do not need to understand the whole system. They need to know the next smallest thing. And then the next. And then the next. Until the shape of the system collapses into something they can hold in their head.

They carry a physical notebook. Every Signal Runner does. Not because it is efficient. Because it is the thing they can hand to the next one when they stop.

**In the story:** Vess is a Signal Runner. She did not know it. She reinvented the method badly and then found it in a box in Sounding's handwriting. And then she found the second half of it on a landing. And then she stopped being a runner and became a person who sits on floors and asks whether people are alright.

**Relationship to other archetypes:** The Signal Runner is the Line's inheritance from Compile. The Netrunner is the Line's tradition. The Signal Runner is the Line's conscience.

## ## THE SIX ARCHETYPES TOGETHER

### CARTOGRAPHER
**Focus:** Intelligence & Body  
**The Thing They Do:** Maps spaces by sound and touch. Reveals structure at a glance.

### INTERFACE ROGUE
**Focus:** Technical & Reflexes  
**The Thing They Do:** Bypasses electronics. Overclocks against cyberware.

### REGULATOR
**Focus:** Cool & Reflexes  
**The Thing They Do:** Clears panic. Holds the room. Is the anchor.

### ADVOCATE
**Focus:** Cool & Technical  
**The Thing They Do:** Reads wants. Spoofs credentials. Takes control of a room.

### NETRUNNER
**Focus:** Technical & Intelligence  
**The Thing They Do:** Wears the deck. Interrogates systems from inside the rendering.

### SIGNAL RUNNER
**Focus:** Intelligence & Cool  
**The Thing They Do:** Refuses the rendering. Asks one question at a time. Finds the truth the deck hides.

## WHY THESE TWO COMPLETE THE SET

**The Cartographer** covers space.

**The Interface Rogue** covers hardware.

**The Regulator** covers the body.

**The Advocate** covers the room.

**The Netrunner** covers the system from inside.

**The Signal Runner** covers the system from outside.

And the Signal Runner is the one who asks the question the system cannot answer.

---

## THE CORE RULE OF THREE (for all archetypes)

Each archetype follows the Rule of Three.

**Memory Anchor** — one thing that keeps you grounded. A photograph. A name. A room. A question. Something you carry that reminds you who you are.

**Gear ×3** — three pieces of equipment. One is your specialty. One is your fallback. One is personal.

**Cyberware ×2** — two implants. One is functional. One is personal. Not every archetype requires cyberware. The Signal Runner may carry none.

**Always-On Specialty Tag** — one permanent ability that defines your approach. It never turns off. It is who you are.

---

## THE SIGNAL RUNNER'S MEMORY ANCHOR

Every Signal Runner has one. It is the thing that keeps them asking.

For Compile, it was the braille display and the notebook and the refusal to wait for hardware that was never coming.

For Sounding, it was the question he asked at every door and the notebook he carried and the bag he left in a corner when he stopped.

For Vess, it was the file with fourteen lines in it. And then she gave it away.

And when she had nothing on her desk, she had the question.

*Are you alright.*

---

## THE NETRUNNER'S MEMORY ANCHOR

A Netrunner's anchor is the thing that keeps them from trusting the rendering.

Some carry a physical object — a key, a card, a dead terminal, a piece of hardware they can hold in their hand. Something real. Something the rendering cannot fake.

Some carry a person — a partner, a handler, someone who verifies what the deck shows them.

Some carry a rule. A discipline. A line from the manual that they repeat to themselves every time the rendering is beautiful and confident and wrong.

*Establish the relationship before you trust the picture.*
