# SIGNAL RUNNERS — WORKING LORE REFERENCE

*A developing worldbuilding reference for CAPSLOCK.*

This document gathers the current ideas surrounding the **Signal Runner** archetype, the **Dead Reckoning Line**, **Compile**, and the role of Signal Runners within the wider CAPSLOCK setting.

It is **not definitive canon**. It is a working consolidation: some elements are established, some are provisional, and some are deliberately left open for further development. Mechanics may change as the wider CAPSLOCK system develops.

The purpose of this document is to establish the **shape, identity, mythology, and mechanical direction** of Signal Runners without pretending that the world has stopped evolving.

---

## 1. What Is a Signal Runner?

A **Signal Runner** is CAPSLOCK's answer to the familiar neon-soaked netrunner cliché.

They do not navigate cyberspace through immersive virtual-reality goggles or elaborate visual interfaces. They work through terminals, speech, tactile interfaces, acoustic feedback, disciplined procedures, and highly efficient code.

Most Signal Runners are blind or low-vision hackers. Some sighted hackers deliberately abandon visual interfaces when working and adopt Signal Runner methods. What defines the archetype is therefore not simply eyesight. It is a **way of interacting with systems**.

A conventional netrunner may experience the net as a rendered environment.

A Signal Runner experiences it as **structure**.

They care about topology, state, relationships, inputs, outputs, constraints, and evidence. They strip away presentation until the underlying system is exposed.

In the sprawl, they have become closely associated with **Rust programming**: memory safety, explicit ownership, zero-cost abstractions, concurrency, and systems that demand precision rather than assumption.

But Signal Runners do not primarily think of themselves as network trespassers.

They are **code hackers**.

They hack at the walls surrounding confusion, misunderstanding, and ignorance.

They break open systems whose complexity has been allowed to become a barrier. They examine code that has been deliberately made opaque. They crack the shells and shields of systems designed to exclude, exploit, or oppress.

Their detractors see something different.

To them, Signal Runners are dangerous ghosts in the machine: people entering territories where they have no business being.

Both descriptions are part of the legend.

## 2. Origins — The Bare-Metal Minimalist

Signal Runners did not begin as a political movement or a subculture.

They began with a practical problem.

Full-sensory rigs gave Megacorps another surface to attack: the user's perception.

Photosensitive lockouts, visual overload, strobing ICE, disorienting displays, and other forms of sensory disruption were often presented as security features. But much of modern ICE had been designed around an assumption that its target would be **looking**.

Signal Runners built around the problem instead of waiting for the problem to be solved.

Some had low vision.

Some had no useful vision.

Some simply discovered that visual interfaces introduced more noise than information.

They dropped the video layer first.

Then they began questioning the assumptions underneath it.

What remained was the net stripped down to its underlying form:

**structure rather than spectacle.**

For years, the city treated them as a curiosity — *the blind kids with the cane-decks.*

Then some of the largest ICE systems in the sprawl began falling to intrusions that their visual logs could not adequately explain.

The joke became less funny.

The Signal Runner advantage was never deliberately designed as an anti-visual weapon. It emerged as a consequence of building systems for bodies and working methods that Megacorp security architects had largely ignored.

Their supposed limitation became an unexpected advantage.

## 3. The Dead Reckoning Line

Signal Runners do not belong to a single Megacorp, gang, or Safe Zone.

Instead, many of them participate in something the sprawl calls the **Dead Reckoning Line**.

The Line is not an organization in the conventional sense.

It is an informal and deliberately undocumented network of:

* safehouses
* borrowed server racks
* trusted terminals
* temporary workspaces
* technical contacts
* favors
* reputation
* word-of-mouth introductions

There is no membership card.

There is no central server.

There is no headquarters that can be raided and no master database that contains every Runner.

You get onto the Line because someone already on it decides to vouch for you.

You remain on it by making sure that person's trust was not misplaced.

The Line therefore runs on something more valuable than credentials:

**reputation under pressure.**

## 4. RUSTCORE

To a conventional netrunner, **RUSTCORE** is an obstacle.

To a Signal Runner, it looks strangely familiar.

RUSTCORE is built around a philosophy they already understand:

* the system refuses to accept what has not been verified;
* assumptions have consequences;
* ownership matters;
* borrowing matters;
* state matters;
* precision matters;
* discipline is part of the solution.

Where another hacker fights that discipline, a Signal Runner may use it as leverage.

Ownership and borrowing are not merely programming concepts to them. They are part of a broader mental model:

**know what you have, know who controls it, know what you are allowed to do with it, and verify before you proceed.**

This is one reason Signal Runners have developed a reputation for reaching places inside RUSTCORE infrastructure that other hackers struggle to understand.

They were never trying to escape the discipline.

They learned to operate inside it.

## 5. Culture and Values

Signal Runner culture is not completely unified. The following are recurring tendencies rather than universal rules.

### The Pragmatist

Heavy anti-static techwear.

Deep pockets for physical hardware tokens.

Equipment chosen for function rather than appearance.

An opaque visor may be worn not because the wearer needs it, but because it blocks the neon that everyone else insists on displaying.

### The Elitist Artisan

Some Signal Runners have considerable contempt for purely visual hacking rigs and inexperienced "script kiddies."

Their standard is simple:

If you cannot understand what your program is doing, you are not yet running.

If you cannot trace a value's lifetime, follow the system's state, or explain why an operation is safe, you are still a tourist.

This attitude is not universally shared, and it is one source of friction within the wider Runner community.

### The Vouch System

There are no resumes on the Dead Reckoning Line.

No recruitment advertisements.

No official certification.

Someone already on the Line decides whether you are worth trusting.

Their reputation is then partially tied to yours.

## 6. Signature Gear and Interfaces

### The Braille Matrix

A tactile interface capable of representing enormous amounts of structural information through rapidly changing physical patterns.

The exact technology remains intentionally under development.

The important idea is that code structure can be **felt**, not merely displayed.

### Acoustic Pingers

Spatialized and multi-tonal audio cues representing system topology.

A Runner can interpret changing tones and spatial relationships in much the same conceptual way that sonar reveals the shape of an unseen environment.

### The Rust Deck

A ruggedized computing platform with little or no conventional video output.

Processing power is prioritized for:

* computation
* error detection
* verification
* communications
* heat management
* reliability

The Rust Deck is less a magical cyberdeck than an extreme evolution of a systems programmer's workstation.

### The Whisper Line

A bone-conduction communications channel routed beneath the jaw rather than over the ears.

The design solves a practical problem:

A Runner should not have to choose between hearing the network and hearing the physical world around them.

## 7. Mechanics Flavor

These concepts are currently being explored as the mechanical expression of Signal Runner fiction.

### Compile-Time Safety

Preparation is part of the action.

A Runner may spend considerable time establishing assumptions, constraints, and safeguards before executing a plan.

Once the system has been verified, the resulting operation is difficult to disrupt without confronting the underlying logic.

The discipline is the defense.

### Visual ICE Resistance

Some ICE relies on visual disruption:

* strobing
* flashing patterns
* visual overload
* disorienting displays
* blinding effects

These attacks are far less useful against someone whose interface does not depend upon seeing them.

This is not necessarily a supernatural immunity.

It is an interface mismatch.

The attack is aimed at a layer the Runner has already discarded.

### Panic State

A failed program can enter a controlled panic state.

The deck terminates the operation, isolates affected memory, and drops the connection.

The Runner may lose the run.

They should not lose themselves with it.

## 8. The CLI Arsenal

Signal Runners make heavy use of ordinary command-line tools. Their reputation comes partly from what they can accomplish with tools that other hackers consider mundane.

### `rg` — ripgrep

Fast structural and textual searches through hostile source trees, logs, configuration, and code.

### `jq`

Filtering and interrogating chaotic JSON telemetry and machine-generated data.

### `fd`

Rapidly locating files and structures across unfamiliar systems.

### `strace` and `perf`

Observing what a running process actually does rather than trusting what its interface claims it does.

### `lldb` and `rust-lldb`

Inspecting program state and behavior directly.

The tools themselves are not magical.

The important part is the **method** behind them.

Signal Runners learn to ask increasingly precise questions:

**Question → Query → Answer → Narrow → Trace → Verify**

The complete methodology is preserved in the **RUSTCORE Field Manual**, also known as the **BVI Rust Programmer's Manual**.

The Field Manual is both a technical document and, increasingly, a piece of Line mythology.

## 9. The Five Approaches

The Five Approaches are adapted from an outside diceless system and integrated into CAPSLOCK's Rule of Three.

They are not numerical statistics.

They describe the **ways a character tends to approach a problem**.

### BODY

Physical endurance, balance, movement, spatial feedback, and physical interaction with the environment.

### REFLEXES

Speed, coordination, timing, and immediate reaction.

### TECHNICAL

Tools, electronics, cyberware, machines, and technical systems.

### INTELLIGENCE

Memory, pattern recognition, reasoning, mapping, and structural understanding.

### COOL

Composure, social confidence, pressure resistance, and the ability to remain functional when circumstances become chaotic.

A character has two Focus Approaches.

When an action falls within those two approaches, a **Yes, but** can be upgraded to a clean **Yes**.

This can stack with Style's own upgrade when both apply.

---

## 10. Sensory Clues

The Five Approaches connect directly to CAPSLOCK's Rule of Threes.

Each location can contain three sensory clues:

* one light
* one sound
* one smell

The Approaches provide different ways of interpreting those clues.

**Intelligence** reveals patterns and relationships.

**Reflexes** reveal immediate physical changes.

**Body** reveals spatial and physical feedback.

**Technical** reveals information through equipment.

**Cool** reveals information through people.

A character's Focus Approach can provide an additional sensory anchor.

This is still an area of active development rather than fixed doctrine.

---

## 11. The Sensory Loop

The **Sensory Loop** is being explored as a fourth companion to the core PICO·ATTO backbone.

Its current form is:

**Explore → Get a sensory clue → State an action → Clock Draw → Handle the consequence → Continue**

The consequence may produce a **Glitch** or a **Condition**.

The loop is intended to make movement through the world feel grounded in what the character can actually perceive rather than in abstract descriptions of what a camera would show them.

---

## 12. Conditions

Conditions are an optional harder consequence alongside **Glitched Gear**.

A failed action may produce a single condition such as:

* Disoriented
* Exhausted
* Injured
* Overloaded
* Exposed

Conditions are not intended to function as a countdown meter.

They are narrative problems.

They are cleared through an appropriate narrative solution rather than by simply waiting for a timer to expire.

# 13. The Archetypes

The Archetypes describe **how a character tends to solve problems**.

They are not disability classes, professions, or fixed character roles.

A Signal Runner may use any Archetype.

A sighted character may use any Archetype.

A character may begin with one Archetype and gradually develop another as experience changes what they are capable of.

Each Archetype currently follows the CAPSLOCK Rule of Three:

* one Memory Anchor
* three Gear items
* two Cyberware items
* one Always-On specialty

The equipment examples below are deliberately broad.

Accessibility technologies may appear among them, but they are not treated as special equipment for disabled characters. A street GPS, text-to-speech system, tactile display, bone-conduction headset, or audio navigation system can be useful to almost anyone.

The important question is not:

**"Who is this equipment for?"**

It is:

**"What does this equipment let the character do?"**

---

## CARTOGRAPHER

*"The room was already mapped before I stepped in."*

**Focus:** Intelligence & Body

The Cartographer understands spaces.

They notice relationships between locations, routes, boundaries, entrances, exits, obstacles, and changes in terrain.

They are good at building a mental model of a place and using that model to move through it.

**Gear:**

* street-navigation GPS
* environmental scanner
* compact mapping terminal

A Cartographer might use visual maps, spoken directions, tactile information, augmented reality, or a combination of them. The technology is secondary to the ability to construct and use a reliable map.

**Cyberware:**

* Subdermal Compass — provides a constant sense of direction.
* Echo-Sense — interprets reflected sound and environmental information to construct spatial information.

**Always-On — Structural Echo**

Once per scene, reveal the major structure of a space at once: exits, hazards, routes, and important spatial relationships.

The exact form of the information depends on the character's interface.

---

## INTERFACE ROGUE

*"Electronics forget they were ever locked."*

**Focus:** Technical & Reflexes

The Interface Rogue specializes in machines.

They understand controls, protocols, interfaces, electronics, cyberware, and the small weaknesses that appear when systems have to communicate with one another.

They are comfortable taking an unfamiliar device and figuring out how to make it respond.

**Gear:**

* diagnostic multitool
* portable terminal
* adaptive control interface

An adaptive interface might be a visual display, speech-driven system, tactile controller, gesture interface, or something stranger.

**Cyberware:**

* Neural Overclock
* Reflex-Synced Fingers

**Always-On — Overclock**

Electronic or cyberware-based Enforcers and Hunters operate at one lower Difficulty band against the Interface Rogue.

---

## REGULATOR

*"Panic is just noise, and noise passes."*

**Focus:** Cool & Reflexes

The Regulator specializes in maintaining function under pressure.

They understand attention, stress, distraction, sensory overload, and the difference between reacting and responding.

Their equipment often reduces unnecessary information rather than adding more of it.

**Gear:**

* text-to-speech or speech-processing system
* sensory filter
* grounding or stabilization device

A Regulator might deliberately simplify an interface, reduce visual clutter, filter audio, magnify important signals, convert information into speech, or use tactile feedback.

Accessibility technology fits naturally here because technologies designed to control information density are useful far beyond disability.

**Cyberware:**

* Adrenal Regulator
* Sensory Limiter

**Always-On — Anchor**

Once per scene, clear a Condition rooted in stress, panic, or sensory overload, either belonging to the Regulator or an ally.

---

## ADVOCATE

*"Everyone in the room already told you what they want, if you listened."*

**Focus:** Cool & Technical

The Advocate specializes in people, communication, identity, and access.

They understand that information is often hidden not behind encryption but behind social rules.

They know how systems of authority work, who is allowed through a door, which credentials matter, and what people actually need before they will cooperate.

**Gear:**

* identity and credential kit
* communications terminal
* translation or voice-processing system

These tools might include a voice modifier, translation engine, text-to-speech system, document reader, encrypted communicator, or other technology that changes how information moves between people.

**Cyberware:**

* Emotional Resonance Reader
* Vocal Modulator

**Always-On — Command**

Once per scene, when taking control of a social interaction, determine the NPC's underlying want, fear, or need before playing the interaction out.

---

## Archetypes Are Not Locked Roles

The Archetypes are deliberately porous.

A Cartographer may become an Interface Rogue.

An Advocate may become a Regulator.

A Signal Runner may combine elements of all four.

A character's equipment can also change the way an Archetype manifests.

A street GPS is not inherently "Cartographer gear."

A text-to-speech system is not inherently "blind gear."

A tactile display is not inherently "accessibility equipment."

The same technology can serve different purposes for different characters.

The Archetype describes the **capability and approach**.

The Gear describes the **means**.

The character's body, preferences, training, culture, and technology determine how those means are actually experienced.

This keeps accessibility inside the world rather than putting it in a separate category.

Signal Runners simply pushed this principle much further than most people in the sprawl.

# 14. The Fluid Types

The Archetypes describe recognizable specializations.

The **Fluid Types** describe broader identities that become available as a character develops greater proficiency.

They are not necessarily starting classes.

A character can become a Fluid Type through experience, then append an Archetype to that identity.

The two current Fluid Types are:

## NETRUNNER

**Focus:** Technical & Intelligence

**The Thing They Do:**

They wear the deck.

They interrogate systems from inside the rendering.

The Netrunner accepts the interface and learns to dominate it.

---

## SIGNAL RUNNER

**Focus:** Intelligence & Cool

**The Thing They Do:**

They refuse the rendering.

They ask one question at a time.

They find the truth the deck hides.

The Signal Runner is less concerned with entering the system's presentation layer and more concerned with discovering the structure underneath it.

---

The distinction between Netrunner and Signal Runner is therefore not simply about blindness, technology, or equipment.

It is philosophical.

**The Netrunner enters the system's world.**

**The Signal Runner questions the system's assumptions.**

A character may develop toward either identity, move between them, or combine them with an Archetype as their skills develop.

This fluidity is intentional and remains subject to further design.

---

# 15. Helping Another Character — Duet Mode

With a second player, one character can assist another by describing **how** they help.

If the assistance meaningfully fits the situation, the helped character's next **Yes, but** can be upgraded to a clean **Yes**.

This mirrors the upgrade normally provided by Style, but makes it possible for another character to earn that advantage through cooperation.

---

# 16. Equipment as Permission

Equipment does not primarily exist to add numerical bonuses.

It provides **narrative permission**.

The right tool can change:

**"You can't."**

into:

**"You can."**

A long cane, tactile tool, accessible terminal, braille display, or audio mapping device therefore does not need a special accessibility subsystem.

It simply belongs in the Gear system.

This principle is important to Signal Runner worldbuilding:

The city may be technologically advanced without assuming that everyone experiences that technology through the same sensory channel.

Accessibility is therefore not merely an accommodation added to the world.

It can become a source of capability.

# 17. The Legend of Compile

Every Signal Runner on the Dead Reckoning Line knows the name:

**Compile.**

Her real name is unknown.

Her status is unknown.

Her last verified sighting was eleven years ago.

She is variously described as:

* founder
* ghost
* teacher
* hacker
* myth
* author of the RUSTCORE Field Manual

The Line has never settled on which description is correct.

Compile was blind before the first Signal Runner decks existed.

Her original toolkit was remarkably ordinary:

a terminal,

a braille display,

and an increasingly refined habit of asking one precise question at a time.

That habit eventually became a formal method:

**Question → Query → Answer → Narrow → Trace → Verify**

Every apprentice on the Line is eventually taught it.

---

## 18. The RUSTCORE Vault

Compile's most famous legend is the **RUSTCORE Vault job**.

The vault was protected by visual-disruption ICE.

The expected solution was to find a vulnerability in the vault's defenses.

Compile apparently did something else.

She removed the eye from the problem.

Instead of trying to defeat the visual system, she traced the assumptions the vault itself was making about its operator.

The legend says she discovered that the vault's security was not merely protecting a system.

It was making assumptions about **who was looking at it**.

Compile followed those assumptions until the vault's own logic became the route through its defenses.

She reportedly cracked the vault without ever confronting its visual layer directly.

The details have become increasingly mythologized, and the surviving accounts do not completely agree.

That uncertainty is part of the story.

---

## 19. Compile's Disappearance

Compile disappeared not long after the Vault job.

No body was found.

No confirmed transmission followed.

No one has established whether she died, escaped, went underground, or simply decided that the Line should stop looking for her.

The only surviving trace attributed to her is a notebook line:

*"The next question is outside this system."*

The Line has never agreed on what it means.

More importantly, the Line has never treated her disappearance as proof of anything.

One phrase became associated with the incident:

**Her disappearance is not evidence.**

That sentence has gradually become part of Signal Runner culture.

---

## 20. Compile's Creed

There are several versions of Compile's supposed creed.

None is considered authoritative.

The version repeated most often is approximately:

*"Ask the smallest question that gets you closer. Build each next question on the last answer. Never mistake the system's silence for it hiding from you. It may simply need the right question."*

Whether Compile actually said those exact words is unknown.

That uncertainty has not stopped people from teaching them.

---

## 21. The Compile Archives

Compile's complete story is larger than this document.

Current plans treat it as several related bodies of material:

* a three-part narrative trilogy covering her major story;
* a four-part deep-lore series exploring her influences and toolkit;
* a recovered early-workshop transcript concerning text-first web research;
* the RUSTCORE Field Manual;
* additional fragments that may or may not be authentic Compile material.

This document does not attempt to reproduce those works.

It only establishes enough of Compile's legend for the Signal Runner archetype to stand on its own.

# 22. World Constants This Archetype Touches

Signal Runner lore intersects with several existing CAPSLOCK concepts.

### RUSTCORE

The compiler-Megacorp whose philosophy of ownership, borrowing, verification, and precision provides a natural environment for Signal Runner stories.

### ICE

Security systems designed to prevent intrusion.

Signal Runners are particularly associated with resistance to visual-disruption ICE, not because of a magical immunity, but because their primary interface does not depend on the visual layer being attacked.

### The Segfault Skulls

One of the sprawl's oldest gangs.

Their culture is built around legacy systems, unverified code, improvisation, and inherited infrastructure.

They represent a useful counterpoint to the discipline associated with RUSTCORE and Signal Runner practice.

### Quest Deconstruction

The broader CAPSLOCK framework for breaking situations into actionable elements.

### Watcher, Enforcer, Hunter

The existing opposition framework into which Signal Runner mechanics can be integrated.

Signal Runner mechanics are intended to **plug into** these systems rather than replace them.

# 23. What Is Established — and What Is Still Open

This document currently establishes the following broad ideas:

* Signal Runners are a distinct hacker culture.
* Their identity is based on method and interface, not simply blindness.
* The Dead Reckoning Line is their informal network of trust.
* RUSTCORE is central to their technical mythology.
* Compile is the central legendary figure.
* Question-driven investigation is their defining methodology.
* Their equipment emphasizes tactile, acoustic, and command-line interaction.
* Their mechanics should reinforce accessibility as capability rather than treating it as a penalty.
* Signal Runner identity can coexist with broader CAPSLOCK Archetypes and Fluid Types.

The following remain deliberately open:

* the exact history of the first Signal Runner;
* how much of Compile's story is true;
* the precise technology behind the Braille Matrix;
* the exact relationship between RUSTCORE and the Dead Reckoning Line;
* whether Signal Runners constitute a genuine subculture or merely a label applied by outsiders;
* how common Signal Runners actually are;
* the precise mechanical implementation of the Five Approaches;
* the final relationship between Netrunners and Signal Runners;
* whether the Dead Reckoning Line has deeper organization than anyone admits.

Those unanswered questions are not necessarily gaps.

Some of them may be **useful mysteries**.

The setting does not have to know everything about itself yet.
