# SIGNAL RUNNERS — EXPANDED GEAR & CYBERWARE

*An addendum to the Signal Runners lore. More options per archetype, each one a cyberpunk reimagining of a real category of assistive technology — screen readers, ultrasonic wayfinding wearables, refreshable tactile displays, bone-conduction audio, and more — rebuilt as original CAPSLOCK gear rather than literal branded equipment.*

Pick freely across an archetype's list — nothing here is meant to replace the original three Gear Slots or two Cyberware Tags from the core lore, just to widen what fills them.

## CARTOGRAPHER — Focus: Intelligence & Body

**Design note:** built around real-world wayfinding tools — ultrasonic obstacle-sensing wristbands, audio-based turn-by-turn navigation, and refreshable tactile graphics displays — reimagined as street-grade and black-market hardware.

### Gear
- **Long cane, hidden-edge model** *(original)* — folds to a bladed core when the situation calls for it.
- **Audio mapping device** *(original)* — narrates a space's layout in real time as you move through it.
- **Tactile floor-plan overlay** *(original)* — a static pin-array sheet of a known building, memorized by touch before the job starts.
- **Sonar bangle** — a wrist-worn ultrasonic emitter that pulses harder and faster the closer an obstacle gets, silent to anyone standing next to you.
- **Pocket wayfinder** — a palm-sized beacon unit that chimes directional cues through a bone-conduction feed, built for navigating a city block at a dead run without breaking stride.
- **Tactile graphics slate** — a refreshable pin-array display that renders schematics, blueprints, or a hastily-scanned map as raised geometry under the fingertips.

### Cyberware
- **Subdermal Compass** *(original)* — always know true bearing.
- **Echo-Sense** *(original)* — map a space by sound alone.
- **Bone-relay sonar** — an implanted click emitter paired with bone-conduction feedback, running a constant low-level echolocation loop you stop consciously noticing within a week of installation.
- **Proprioceptive overlay** — fuses inner-ear balance data with whatever terrain map you're currently building, so uneven ground, stairs, and debris stop being a surprise your feet have to solve on the spot.

# ASSISTIVE TECHNOLOGY — THE SHARED TOOLKIT

These items are available across archetypes. They are not exclusive to Signal Runners, but Signal Runners are the ones who use them most. The sprawl does not make special equipment for blind people. It makes equipment for people who want to work without looking. And blind people have been using it for decades.

---

## Screen Readers and Audio Interfaces

**VoiceWeave** — A bone-conduction auditory interface that reads text, system output, and environmental metadata aloud in a synthetic voice the user has customized over years. Reads at up to 900 words per minute. The voice has a name. The user chose it. (Real-world analog: VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA, JAWS.)

**Whisper** — A subvocal audio interface. Reads system output directly into the user's auditory cortex via bone conduction. Silent to everyone else in the room. The user hears the system. The room hears nothing.

**Verse** — A screen reader that doesn't just read. It interprets. It tells you what a screen *is* — a menu, a warning, a dead end, a form that wants three fields. It gives you the shape before the content. (Real-world analog: advanced screen reader with semantic layer.)

**Tacet** — A screen reader that reads *silences*. It tells you what a system is not saying. What fields are empty. What processes are missing. What the form does not ask for.

**Aria** — A voice interface that speaks in a register the user has trained. Not a synthetic voice. A *persona*. Some users pick a calm voice. Some pick a voice that sounds like a friend. Some pick a voice that sounds like nobody they have ever met, because they do not want to be comforted. They want to be told.

**Loop** — A short-loop auditory buffer. Repeats the last ten seconds of any audio the user has heard. Useful for catching what you missed. Dangerous for people who get stuck.

---

## Braille and Tactile Interfaces

**Refreshable Braille Display (RBD)** — A standard tool. A row of pins that rise and fall to render text in braille. The user reads with their fingertips. A good RBD is fast. A great RBD is fast and quiet. (Real-world analog: BrailleNote, HumanWare, standard refreshable displays.)

**Full-Page Braille Slate** — A larger display that renders a full page of text at once. Expensive. Heavy. Rare. The people who carry them do not leave them behind.

**Tactile Floor-Plan Overlay** — A thin mat or board that renders a space as raised lines and textures. The user reads the space with their feet or hands. (Real-world analog: tactile maps, relief maps.)

**Braille Tattoo** — Dermal braille. Raised dots under the skin of the forearm or wrist. Renders short messages — a name, a number, a warning. Permanent. Cannot be removed. Cannot be read by anyone who does not know braille. (Fictional.)

**Dermal Braille Patch** — A temporary braille patch applied to the skin. Renders live data — system status, proximity alerts, incoming messages. Peels off. Disposable. Expensive. (Fictional.)

**Palm Reader** — A small tactile device held in the palm. Renders text in braille on a refreshable surface. The user holds it the way you hold a phone. Reads by touch. Does not need to be seen.

---

## Proximity and Navigation

**Sunu-Style Sonar Band** — A wristband that emits ultrasonic pulses and returns haptic feedback. The user feels objects before they reach them. Range: about three metres. The band vibrates faster as the user gets closer to an obstacle. (Real-world analog: Sunu Band, BuzzClip, smart canes.)

**Smart Cane** — A cane with ultrasonic sensors and haptic feedback in the handle. Detects obstacles above knee level — the things a standard cane misses. Vibrates when it finds something. (Real-world analog: WeWALK, smart canes.)

**Subdermal Compass** — A micro-implant at the base of the skull that gives the user a constant sense of true bearing. They always know which way is north. No interface. No device. Just knowing. (Fictional. Listed under Cartographer, but available to others.)

**Echo-Sense** — A cyberware implant that maps a space by sound alone. The user clicks or hums and the implant reads the returning echo. The shape of the room appears in their mind — not as an image, as a *sense of space*. (Fictional.)

**Wayfinder** — A navigation implant that guides the user through a known route via haptic pulses on the skin. Left hand pulses: turn left. Right hand: turn right. Both hands: stop. A pattern on the back: you have arrived.

**Trace** — A short-range position tracker. Marks a location — a doorway, a corner, a person — with a tiny beacon. The user can always find their way back to it. Works up to one kilometre.

---

## Text and Image Access

**OcuRead** — A handheld scanner that reads printed text aloud. Point it at a page. It reads. (Real-world analog: OCR readers, Seeing AI, KNFB Reader.)

**Scene Describe** — A camera-and-audio device that describes a scene aloud. *There is a person standing by the counter. They are wearing a coat. They are holding something in their left hand.* (Real-world analog: Seeing AI scene description, Be My Eyes.)

**Human Bridge** — A service, not a device. A network of sighted volunteers who describe what a camera sees. The user points a camera. A stranger tells them what is there. (Real-world analog: Be My Eyes, Aira.)

**Tactile Raster** — A device that converts images into tactile output. A photograph becomes a set of raised lines and textures. The user reads the image with their hands. Slow. Imprecise. Better than nothing. (Real-world analog: tactile graphics embossers.)

---

## Undistracted-Sight Tools

These are for sighted users who choose to work without visual rendering. They are not blind. They have chosen the discipline.

**Blinders** — A pair of opaque lenses or a light-blocking hood. Removes the visual channel. Forces the user to work by touch and sound. Uncomfortable. Effective. The first hour is the hardest.

**Visual Squelch** — A cyberware implant that temporarily suppresses visual cortex input. The user can still see, but the brain stops rendering. Everything becomes a blur of colour and movement. Useful for Netrunners who want to work without the deck's visual layer.

**White Room** — A physical space with no visual features. No windows. No decorations. No colour. The user works in the room and the room gives them nothing to look at. The Line's oldest runners use these for deep work.

# SIGNAL RUNNER — DEEP DIVE

## Three Populations, One Toolkit

The Signal Runner toolkit serves three populations.

**Blind users.** Born blind or blinded. No useful retinal implant. No cortical patch. No neural rendering. The sprawl's medical system has been promising next-generation hardware for decades. It has not arrived. They stopped waiting.

**Low-vision users.** Partial sight. Enough to navigate, not enough to trust. Some use visual aids. Some use audio and tactile. Some use both. They calibrate their toolkit to their remaining vision — and to their need to work without it when the work demands precision.

**Undistracted-sight users.** Fully sighted. Chose to work without visual rendering. Not because they cannot see. Because they have learned — through training, through experience, through the Line's discipline — that the visual channel is a liability. They can see. They choose not to look. The Line calls this *working blind* even though the person is not blind.

All three use the same tools. The reasons differ. The discipline does not.

# SIGNAL RUNNER — TERMINAL MASTERY

The Signal Runner archetype is blind, low vision or choosing not to use GUI or their sight for the job. Their instrument is the terminal. Their craft is the command line.

---

## The Signal Runner's Instrument

The Signal Runner works at a terminal.

Not a deck. Not a rendering. Not a visual interface. A terminal. A shell. A command line. A braille display and a keyboard and a method.

They are masters of the console. They read systems the way other runners read a cityscape — through text, through output, through the shape of data as it appears in a shell.

**Bash.** The shell. The foundation. Every Signal Runner knows bash the way a driver knows the route.

**grep.** The search tool. The first query. The smallest question. `grep -r "function_name" /path` is the Signal Runner's opening move.

## The Signal Runner's Workflow

A Signal Runner sits at a terminal.

They run `file` on the unknown object. They run `strings` on the binary. They `grep` for a function name. They `rg` through a directory. They `find` for recently modified files. They `strace` a running process. They `lsof` to see what is open. They `ss` to see what is connected.

They read the output on a braille display. They hear the output through a screen reader. They feel the shape of the system in text.

They ask one question. They get one answer. They ask the next.

They do not see the system. They read it.

## Why the Terminal

The terminal is not a compromise. It is a discipline.

A visual rendering gives you a model. A terminal gives you the thing itself. `ls` does not show you a beautiful tree. It shows you the files. `ps` does not show you a cityscape. It shows you the processes. `grep` does not show you a highlighted pathway. It shows you the lines that match.

The terminal does not render. It reports.

And a report can be verified. A rendering can only be trusted.

This is why Compile used a terminal. This is why the Signal Runner uses a terminal. Not because they cannot see. Because they refuse to be shown.

## The Line's Argument

The Line has two traditions and they argue constantly.

The **Netrunner** tradition says: use the deck. Use the rendering. Use the visual model. It is faster. It is more powerful. It is the standard tool of the trade.

The **Signal Runner** tradition says: the rendering is a model, and a model is not the territory, and a runner who trusts a model is a runner who is about to be wrong.

Both are correct. The Netrunner is faster. The Signal Runner is more precise. The Line teaches both. And the Line's oldest runners will tell you that the best runners use both — a deck for speed, a terminal for truth, and the discipline to know which one they are looking at.
